# For everyone

## 1. The basics

Pack 1125 has a free public website. Think of it as a flyer and a front door. It tells new families who you are, when you meet, and how to join.

The site has no login. It has no roster. It holds no private facts about any child. Private work lives in Scoutbook Plus. That is the official system from Scouting America.

The site costs nothing to run. It lives in one folder. The pack can copy that folder and move it to a new host. You can hand the site to a new volunteer with this guide.

Five facts cover most of what follows:

**1.**  The site is public. Anyone in the world can see each page. Put only what you would pin on a board in a public library.

**2.**  You do not need a parent login on the site. Parents have a login in Scoutbook Plus. They use it for records and advancement.

**3.**  You handle photos with care. You post a photo only when a parent has said yes in writing. You blur each child face. A parent can opt in, opt out, or ask you to take a photo down. One short form covers all three.

**4.**  You are not locked in. The site is plain web pages in one repository. A volunteer with the right skills can move it in an afternoon.

**5.**  Brandon built the site. He does not have to be the only person who can run it. Follow section 7 and the list at the back and a new person can take over.

## 2. What the site is and what it is not

Think of the site as the pack front porch. It is open and welcoming. You do not leave valuables on it.

The site is a place to recruit and to share news with new and current families. It holds the public calendar for pack and den meetings and events. It holds a photo gallery. You post photos there only with permission and with faces blurred. It points families to official forms, the charter, and Scoutbook Plus.

The site is not a membership system. It holds no roster and no family accounts. It does not track advancement. Scoutbook Plus does that job. It is not a photo archive. You never store first, unblurred photos on the site. You do not use it for dues, health forms, or any form with personal data.

The pack, as the site states it: Cub Scout Pack 1125, Leaders and Trailblazers, is a K to 5 pack for boys and girls in Dumfries, Virginia. Future Kings, Inc. charters the pack. The pack sits in the Prince William District of the National Capital Area Council. You meet at 6:30 PM in the Covington-Harper Elementary School auditorium. Pack meetings fall on the 1st Thursday of the month. Den meetings fall on the 3rd Thursday, from 6:30 to 7:30 PM. Pack dues for 2026-27 are $100. The Join page has the facts on help with dues.

The site has 11 pages: Home, About, Join, New Families, Dens, Calendar, Gallery, Resources, FAQ, Contact, and Privacy and Photo Policy. The New Families page has a For Parents part. Current families can find advancement, photos, and the calendar in one place.

## 3. The seven questions

Rich Sackett asked to talk through seven points about the site. You get the short answer here. Part 2 has the steps. Part 3 has the reasons.

### 1. Public or private. Do you need a parent login?

The site stays public. You cannot add a login to this kind of site without a rebuild. You do not need one. Scoutbook Plus gives each parent a safe login for private work. Use this rule: public facts go on the site, private facts go in Scoutbook.

### 2. Photo storage. Where do photos live?

Photos you publish live in the site Gallery. The Gallery held 28 photos when this guide was written. The files sit with the site. They are small web copies with faces blurred. They are not an archive. The leader who took a photo keeps the first copy.

You use one Google Form, the Pack 1125 Photo Permission form. Links sit on the New Families page and the Privacy page. A parent picks opt in, opt out, or remove a photo. A parent fills in one form for each Scout. If you have no permission on file, you do not publish the photo.

No roster facts sit on the site. You never post a Scout full name, school, phone number, or clear face. The site collects nothing. It has no sign ups, no cookies, and no tracking. The only email address on show is the pack address, admin@pack1125.org.

### 5. Hosting. Are you locked in?

No. GitHub Pages hosts the site for free. The site is plain web pages in one repository that the pack controls. You can copy it to a new host. You pay for nothing, so no card can lapse and take the site down.

Brandon runs the site now as the pack website volunteer. Section 5 lists the routine jobs. When he steps back, use section 7 and the handover list at the back. Repository access, domain access, the calendar, the photo form, and the admin@pack1125.org address each pass to a named person.

### 7. Links to Scoutbook Plus for rank work

The links are in place. Scoutbook Plus sits at advancements.scouting.org. You can reach it from the Resources page and from the For Parents part of the New Families page. You never track advancement on the site. Scoutbook holds the official record.

**One choice sits inside question 1.** If the pack wants a private area at some point, put it in a place with real access controls. Scoutbook works. A shared drive with named access works. ## 5. The five jobs that come up

Most website work falls into five jobs. You get the short form here. Part 2 has the full steps for each job.

### Job 1. Check or change an event

Events come from the pack Google Calendar. If you need to change an event, change it in the calendar. Do not ask for a website edit. The site picks up calendar changes at the next build. It builds each day on its own, and it builds again at each site update. Check the live Calendar page in each meeting week. Room and door facts have changed in the past.

### Job 2. Add photos to the gallery

Add a photo only when you have permission on file for each Scout in it. Blur each child face. Strip the hidden location data from the file. Keep the file small. If you do not know if you have permission, do not post the photo. Ask first. Full steps: SOP-03.

### Job 3. A parent opts out or asks you to take a photo down

Do this work the same day. The parent can use the Photo Permission form or send mail to admin@pack1125.org. Take the photo down. Tell the parent the photo is gone. Write down the opt out so no one adds the photo back at a later date. Full steps: SOP-04 and SOP-05.

### Job 4. Fix words, a date, or a fact on a page

Send the website volunteer three things: the page name, the old words word for word, and the new words word for word. Small, clear requests get done fast. When a page changes, the Page updated date on that page changes with it. That date is a check on the work. Full steps: SOP-01.

### Job 5. A page looks broken

Write down what you saw and what device you used. Take a screenshot if you can. Send both facts to the website volunteer. Do not try random fixes. If the whole site will not load, see SOP-09. The pack can reach families by email and Scoutbook while you fix the site. The site helps. It is never the only way you reach families.

## 6. Privacy rules

**The main rule.** If you would not want the words read out loud, with your child's name tied to them, in a room full of strangers, keep them off the site. If you have a doubt, leave the item out and ask the Committee Chair.

Never post these items on the site:

- a Scout full name, school, home address, phone number, or email address;

- a clear photo of a child face, or a photo of a Scout when the parent has not said yes in writing;

- roster lists, den lists with child names, health facts, or advancement records;

- an adult leader phone number or email address when that leader has not said yes.

You can tell any parent who asks: the site has no accounts, no sign up forms, no cookies, and no ads or tracking. It does not collect facts about the people who visit. The full rule sits on the Privacy and Photo Policy page on the site. This guide follows that rule.

Scouting America Youth Protection rules sit above this guide. If this guide clashes with current Scouting America rules or the Guide to Safe Scouting, follow the Scouting America rules. Fix this guide after that.

# The work, step by step

Each procedure tells you who owns the job, when you use it, and the steps in order. It ends with a way to tell that you are done. Work through the steps in order the first few times. After that, the headings work as a short list.

## SOP-01. Update content on a page

**Owner:** Website volunteer. The Cubmaster or the Committee Chair says yes to the content.

**Use when:** Words, dates, dues, leaders, or links change.

**1.**  Get the request in writing. You need the page, the old text word for word, and the new text word for word. Send vague requests back. Ask for the exact words.

**2.**  Edit the HTML file for that page in the docs/ folder. Keep the same layout: head, navigation, content, footer. Do not paste layout from Word or from email.

**3.**  Update the Page updated date on the page. Update the match date for that page in sitemap.xml. Use the same new date in both places. The check will fail if the dates do not match.

**4.**  Run the checks on your own machine. See Part 3, section 13. Run python3 scripts/check_site.py and python3 scripts/check_links.py. Fix each fault the checks flag. Do that work before you go on.

**5.**  Open a pull request (PR) for the change. Use a PR for all but a one line typo fix. Wait for the checks on the PR to go green. The checks run a real browser at phone width and at desktop width.

**6.**  Merge the PR after a review. Check that the Build and deploy run went through. Open the live page. Check that you can see the change.

**You are done when:** The live page shows the new content. The deploy run is green. The Page updated date matches sitemap.xml.

**Watch out.** Do not merge a change with a red check just to get it live. A red check means no one has shown that the change is safe. Part 3, section 13 tells the story of the October to November 2026 freeze. Fix the checks first when the checks break.

## SOP-02. Run the calendar

**Owner:** The Cubmaster owns event content. The website volunteer owns the sync.

**Use when:** A meeting or an event is added, moved, or called off.

**1.**  Make the change in the pack Google Calendar. The site does not own event facts.

**2.**  Check the title, the date, the start and end times, and the place. Use the name families know, such as Covington-Harper Elementary auditorium. Put room or door changes in the notes for the event.

**3.**  Wait for the next build, or start a build now. The Build and deploy run starts each day at 06:41 UTC. It starts at each push to main. You can start it from the Actions tab with Run workflow.

**4.**  Open the live Calendar page. Check that the event shows up in the right form. The calendar loads when a visitor clicks Show the Calendar. Test that click.

**5.**  If you call off an event close to the date, do not trust the site on its own. Tell families by email or by Scoutbook as well.

**You are done when:** The live Calendar page shows the right event. Families have heard direct word of a late change.

## SOP-03. Add photos to the gallery

**Owner:** Website volunteer, with Cubmaster approval.

**Use when:** You have photos from a pack event that you want to share.

**1.**  Get the photos from the person who took them. That person keeps the first copies. The site holds web copies only.

**2.**  Check permission. Each Scout in a photo must have an opt in reply on file from the Photo Permission form. See SOP-04. If you have no reply on file, that photo does not go up.

**3.**  Blur each child face. You make no exception. Blur faces in the back of a shot when you can pick the child out.

**4.**  Strip the file data (EXIF). Strip the location data in each case. Save a JPG copy and a WebP copy. Keep each file under 500 KB. Put the files in docs/images/gallery/.

**5.**  Add the photo to docs/gallery.html. Write a short caption with the activity and the month only. Never pair a photo with a Scout name, school, or den.

**6.**  Run the checks in SOP-01. Open a PR. Check the live gallery on a phone after the deploy.

**You are done when:** The new photos show in the live gallery. Faces are blurred. Captions stay broad. Each Scout in view has permission on file.

**Watch out.** A crop does not stand in for permission. If one child in a group photo has opted out, you have three safe paths. You can blur that child and seek clear advice. You can crop that child out in full. You can skip the photo. The safe default is to skip the photo.

**Owner:** The Committee Chair owns the rule. The website volunteer runs the form.

**Use when:** A family joins, a new school year starts, or a parent changes a choice.

**1.**  Share the form link when a family joins and each fall: https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSfLMYSqmwUZk10rP2-4-G3mQDKNnuH5hnJSFoZZWpIjUpX64g/viewform . You can find the link on the New Families page and on the Privacy page.

**2.**  The form asks for the parent or guardian name and email, the Scout first name and den, and one choice: opt in, opt out, or remove a photo. A parent fills in one form for each Scout.

**3.**  Check the Responses tab on the form before each gallery update. See SOP-03. Check it when a take down request comes in.

**4.**  Act on a take down request the same day. See SOP-05. Write back to the parent and state what you did.

**5.**  Keep a short list of opt outs with the Committee Chair. A new volunteer needs the list, not just the raw form data.

**You are done when:** Each gallery photo has an opt in on file. You have acted on each opt out and each take down, and you have told the parent.

## SOP-05. Handle a privacy fault or a take down request

**Owner:** The website volunteer acts. Tell the Committee Chair the same day.

**Use when:** A parent asks you to take a photo down, or a person spots private facts or a clear face on the site.

**1.**  Act first. Talk after. Take the photo or the facts down at once. You can take a page off the site if you need to. A PR with a green deploy is the fast safe path. For a grave fault, ask the repository owner to act at once.

**2.**  Check the live site. Refresh the page in full. Check that the item is gone. Search the rest of the site for the same photo or text.

**3.**  Tell the Committee Chair the same day. State what went up, how long it was up as far as you know, and what you took down.

**4.**  Write back to the person who told you. Confirm that you took the item down. Keep your words short and say you are sorry. Do not argue about the rule.

**5.**  Write the facts down in the repository. Use an issue or a note in MAINTENANCE.md. A clear note stops the same fault in the future.

**6.**  Work out how the item got through. Did you skip the permission check? Did you miss the file data? Did you upload the wrong file? Tighten that step before the next gallery update.

**You are done when:** The item is gone from the live site and you can prove it. The Chair and the person who told you have heard from you. You have a note on the cause.

**Watch out.** Speed counts more than a neat note here. A fine note next day does not undo a child photo that stayed public through the night.

**Part 3. How the site is set up**

# Tech detail for the person who runs the site

This part is for a reader who knows Git, GitHub, HTML, and the basics of web safety. It sets down what the setup is and why you picked it. A future volunteer should not undo a clear choice by chance and turn it into a fault.

## 10. Layout: a static site

The site is a static website. You write the pages by hand in HTML. The pages share one stylesheet. Fonts sit on the site. The site has no server code, no database, no content system, and no build framework. Pages live in docs/ in the repository dmvthrowers/pack1125-dumfries. GitHub Pages serves the pages.

Why you picked a static site for a Cub Scout pack:

- **Safety.** There is less to patch, less to log in to, and less to hack in the usual ways. You have no WordPress, no add ons, and no admin password for a thief to fish for.

- **Fit.** A pack site gets read far more than it gets written. Static hosting fits that shape well.

- **Cost.** GitHub Pages costs nothing for a public repository. You have no hosting bill, no add on fee, and no bundle that can lapse.

- **Records.** Each change is a Git commit. You can see who changed what and when. You can undo a change in one step. That log is your audit trail.

- **Life span.** Plain HTML lasts. A volunteer in five years can read these pages with no hunt for old tools.

You accept one trade off with eyes open. A static site cannot run logins, accounts, form work, or content for one user. That is why private work sits in Scoutbook Plus. You do not fake that work on this site. If a future need calls for live features, add a new service on the side. The pack looked at a split parent portal test in the past. Do not turn the public site into an app to meet that need.

## 11. Repository layout and page rules

**docs/** holds the live site. Each public page, the stylesheet, the fonts, and the images live here. GitHub Pages serves this folder through the Actions workflow.

**docs/images/gallery/** holds the gallery photos you publish. You store a JPG copy and a WebP copy for each photo. Each file is under 500 KB. You strip the EXIF data and blur faces.

**docs/privacy.html** is the Privacy and Photo Policy page. It states in public the rules the SOPs enforce.

**scripts/check_site.py** checks the site rules. It checks sitemap reach, nav and footer match, and that the Page updated date on a page matches the date in sitemap.xml.

**scripts/check_links.py** checks links that point off the site. Each link must give a live reply (200).

**scripts/sync_calendar.py and scripts/publish_events.py** run the calendar steps at build time. See section 14.

**.github/workflows/** holds the check-site workflow and the Build and deploy workflow. The browser job is pinned to ubuntu-24.04.

**MAINTENANCE.md** is the short note in the repository. This guide is the long form. Keep both up to date.

## 12. Hosting, deploys, and the domain

Hosting: GitHub Pages. Set Build and deployment to GitHub Actions in Settings, then Pages. Do not set it to Deploy from a branch. The Actions path runs the checks before a change can reach the live site. A push that fails the checks does not deploy. Pages serves the last good build. A fault leaves you with a stale site. It does not leave you with a half built site. Vercel plays no part in this site.

The Build and deploy workflow starts in three ways. It starts at each push to main. It starts each day at 06:41 UTC. The daily run is how calendar changes show up when you make no content edit. You can start it by hand from the Actions tab.

Domain state: the site loads under the dmvthrowers.club GitHub Pages path for now. The pack has pack1125.org on file at Squarespace Domains. That address shows a registrar hold page at this time. SOP-07 sets out the full move in steps. It covers the four GitHub Pages apex A records, 185.199.108.153 to 185.199.111.153, the www CNAME to dmvthrowers.github.io, the custom domain step in Pages settings, and HTTPS. ## 13. The checks, and the freeze

Three checks run before a deploy:

- **One.** Site rules, in scripts/check_site.py. Each sitemap page sits in the navigation and in the footer. The Page updated date on a page matches the date in sitemap.xml. A changed page needs a fresh date.

- **Two.** HTML checks and link checks, in scripts/check_links.py. The markup must be valid. Each link that points off the site must give a live reply (200).

- **Three.** Real browser checks, with Playwright. The checks open each page at 360 px (phone) and at 1,280 px (desktop). The page must show no sideways scroll. It must show no grave access faults. It must load no files from off the site. It must show no content safety rule errors.

Case in point, the freeze from October to November 2026. The Build and deploy workflow failed again and again on the same commit from October 24, 2026. The browser job died in seconds. It died before it could test a page. The deploy step got skipped each time. The live site did not update for three weeks. The same checks passed on a local machine for all pages. That fact put the fault in the runner, not in the content. The ubuntu-latest image from GitHub had moved to Ubuntu 26.04. Playwright 1.56.1 cannot install Chromium on that image. The fix came in PR #20. The fix pins the browser job, and that job alone, to ubuntu-24.04 in .github/workflows/check-site.yml. The deploy went green after the merge. That run was the first good deploy since October 24. The live site was back up after that.

**Rule from the freeze.** Do not take out the ubuntu-24.04 pin for the browser job. First show, in the CI run for that PR, that the current Playwright build can install and run on the new image. Do not skip a red browser check to force a deploy. A check that fails shut is the system at work.

Habits that flow from that case: check the Dependabot notes and the workflow notes for this repository each week. A failed run on main for the timed workflow is a real fault. The public site is frozen at that point. Treat two failed daily runs in a row as an event. Work SOP-09.

## 14. Calendar sync, in tech terms

The build takes the Calendar page from the pack Google Calendar. Two scripts run the work: scripts/sync_calendar.py and scripts/publish_events.py. The build reads the secret iCal address for the calendar from the repository secret CALENDAR_ICS_URL. The Google label for that address is Secret address in iCal format.

Points you should keep:

- The secret is a read only feed address. It lives as a GitHub Actions secret and in no other place. You never commit it to the repository. You never print it in logs or in this guide.

- The sync changes the copy of the site for that build and that build alone. It writes no files back to the repository. The repository does not fill up with machine made event churn.

- If the secret is not set, the step does no work. The page falls back to fixed content. The build does not fail. A quiet calendar fault can slip past you. Check the live Calendar page each month. Do not trust the workflow color on its own.

- The Google Calendar view loads on the page when a visitor clicks Show the Calendar. Third party calls stay off the rest of the pages and off the first load. See section 15.

Handover note: to pass the calendar on, you pass the Google Calendar, by transfer or by share access. If you rotate the feed address, you update the CALENDAR_ICS_URL secret in the repository settings.

## 15. Safety model

The safety stance rests on less, not more. The site cannot leak what it does not hold. It cannot break through tools it does not run.

- **Attack space.** No database. No server code. No admin screen. No user accounts. No form work on the site. The Photo Permission form is a Google Form. It sits apart from the site.

- **Content rules.** Each page sends a strict rule for content sources. The rule is default-src 'self'. Scripts, styles, fonts, and images can come from the site and from no place else. The rule blocks frames, objects, and form posts. Fonts sit on the site for the same cause. The one clear exception is the Google Calendar frame. You allow it on the Calendar page and on that page alone. Do not widen the rule for the whole site to fix a one page embed.

- **Tracking.** No cookies. No counts of visits. No ads and no tracking code. The Privacy page states this fact in public. The browser check backs it up with a rule that no file loads from off the site.

- **Less data.** The PII rules in Part 1, section 6 act as safety controls. They are not mere manners. You hold no roster. You pair no Scout full name with a photo. You blur faces. You strip EXIF data, since gallery files can hold GPS points. The one public contact is the role address admin@pack1125.org.

- **Access.** Repository access is the real wall. Write access to dmvthrowers/pack1125-dumfries is, in real terms, the right to publish to the public site. Give access by name. Check the list at each handover and each fall. See SOP-08. Use PRs in place of direct pushes to main. A PR gives you the checks and a fresh pair of eyes.

Risks that stay: a thief who takes a volunteer GitHub account could post bad content. PR review cuts that risk. Git history makes a revert fast. A leak of the calendar feed address would show event facts. Keep the address as a secret and rotate it if it shows up in a log or a note. The registrar account for pack1125.org holds the public name. A pack account with two named managers cuts that risk. See Part 1, section 8.

## 16. Access for all users, speed, and search

- **Access.** Pages use HTML with clear sense marks, a skip link, labels on the navigation, and focus you can see. The browser check blocks a deploy when it finds a grave access fault at phone width or at desktop width. Give alt text with sense on content images. Use alt text with no words (alt="") for pure trim and for trim alone.

- **Speed.** Speed flows from the layout. You serve static HTML, one stylesheet, and fonts cut to size and held on the site. Gallery images stay under 500 KB, with WebP next to JPG. Keep that form. Do not add script packs for tricks a stylesheet can do.

- **Search.** sitemap.xml lists all 11 pages. The dates in the file match the Page updated dates you can see. Canonical tags, Open Graph tags, and breadcrumb JSON-LD sit on each page. After the domain move in SOP-07, update each canonical tag, each sitemap line, and each JSON-LD address to pack1125.org in the same PR. Search lists will take a few weeks to settle after the move.

## 17. How you move the site

You can move the site. That fact was a goal from the start. The whole site, with content, images, and past work, is the Git repository. To move hosts, clone dmvthrowers/pack1125-dumfries. Publish the docs/ folder with a static host. GitHub Pages, Netlify, and Cloudflare Pages all work. A plain web server works. Point the DNS at the new host per SOP-07. Set the CALENDAR_ICS_URL secret in the new place if the calendar steps move with you. You need no more steps than those. You need no database export, no vendor screen, and no licence. A skilled volunteer can make the move in an afternoon. The pack sits no more than an afternoon from a move away from a host, GitHub in that list.

Some items do not move on their own. You hand them over in clear steps. See the back pages. The items are the pack1125.org registrar account, the Google Calendar, the Photo Permission Google Form and the replies to it, the admin@pack1125.org address, and this guide. The site can move. The accounts near it are the handover.
